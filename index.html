How the week runs

The split never changes, which is the point. DSA is a daily habit, not a weekend sprint — that is the single biggest difference between people who clear the coding round and people who keep almost clearing it.

DayTimeBlock
Mon–Fri, morning60 minDSA — that week's pattern, 3–4 problems
Mon–Fri, evening90 minCore topic: read, then build something tiny that proves it
Saturday4 hrProject work + redo every DSA problem you failed this week
Sunday3 hrDesign or LLD practice, write the week's notes, plan next week

Five rules that decide whether this works

  • Give a problem 25 minutes. If it's not moving, read the editorial, understand it fully, then close it and re-solve from an empty file.
  • Re-solve every failed problem after 3 days and again after 10. Spaced repetition is what makes patterns stick.
  • Keep one mistake log file. Every bug, every wrong assumption, one line each. Re-read it every Sunday.
  • Write your notes in your own words. Copy-pasted notes feel productive and teach you nothing.
  • Say your reasoning out loud while you solve, from week one. Interviews grade your narration as much as your code.

The plan

Where to learn each thing

JavaScript

  • Namaste JavaScript (Akshay Saini) for the event loop and closures, visually
  • javascript.info as the reference you actually read end to end
  • You Don't Know JS Yet (Kyle Simpson) for scope, closures, and this
  • MDN for anything you are unsure about mid-build

Node and backend

  • Node.js Design Patterns (Casciaro & Mammino) — streams and async patterns chapters are the valuable part
  • The official Node docs on the event loop, streams, and worker threads
  • Hussein Nasser on backend and database internals
  • OWASP API Security Top 10

Databases

  • Use The Index, Luke — for indexing intuition
  • PostgreSQL docs on EXPLAIN, isolation levels, and MVCC
  • MongoDB University's data modelling course
  • Designing Data-Intensive Applications (Kleppmann) — chapters 1–7 and 11, skim the rest

DSA and design

  • Striver's A2Z DSA sheet or NeetCode 150 — pick one and finish it
  • LeetCode for volume, plus two contests a month from week 8 onward
  • System Design Interview Vol 1 & 2 (Alex Xu)
  • Arpit Bhayani and Gaurav Sen for design intuition; Refactoring Guru for patterns

These are from memory, not a live lookup — check the exact titles and links before you buy anything.

If you have less than five months

Compress to 12 weeks by halving Phase 1 to two weeks (you already write JavaScript daily — keep the async and polyfill weeks, skim the rest), cutting Project 2, and reducing DSA to ~250 problems by dropping the harder DP and graph tiers. Do not cut the mock-interview weeks. They convert more offers per hour than anything else on this page.
